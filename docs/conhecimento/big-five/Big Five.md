# Big Five  
  
## **What is the format of this test, and the rationale for that format?**  
You will soon be presented with 100 phrases that will look like this: “I am sad much of the time” or “I frequently compliment people” and asked to indicate the degree to which you agree or disagree with the phrase as it applies to you. The phrases were derived from a large pool of such items, and sorted statistically into five primary and ten secondary dimensions (the Big Five traits and aspects, respectively). Each of the five primary dimensions is derived from the answer to twenty questions; each of the ten secondary dimensions from ten. Thus, although there is some room for interpretation with regard to each phrase, the scores for each trait and aspect, summed across many items, tend to be quite accurate and stable. Some Big Five personality tests use single adjectives. Others use full questions. Phrases have been identified as a reasonable compromise between the generality of the former and the specificity of the latter.  
## **How many times can I take this test?**  
You can only take the test once. A genuinely accurate personality test has to be taken (and scored) in a standardized manner. The sample of people against whom your scores will be compared only took the test a single time. We have to make the conditions under which you take the test as similar to those of the comparison group as possible, and that means only having you take it once, as well. Furthermore, if you take the test, and then look at your results, and then re-take it, your second results will be subject to biases introduced by your now-expanded knowledge of your personality. That means you will change your responses, in all likelihood, and distort your answers.  
## **What conditions should surround me and what state of mind should I be in to answer these questions?**  
As you can only take the test once, you should answer the 100 questions carefully and thoughtfully. Answer them so they describe you as you are, typically or on average, in your day to day life.  
Please consider the following seriously:  
1. It is best not to take the test under conditions that will make you more self-critical.  
2. If you’re hungry, eat something first, as hunger will make you more negative and judgmental.  
3. If you are feeling unhappier or more anxious than usual, pick another time to complete the questions.  
4. If you are currently depressed or suffering from an anxiety disorder, then your results will be skewed unreasonably toward the negative (so it’s better to wait to assess your personality until you are more stable in your emotional responses).  
5. If you’re rushed, wait to answer the questions until you have time to think.  
6. If you are likely to be interrupted or distracted, wait until you are alone and can work without hurrying or being disrupted.  
If you have satisfied the conditions above, and you are in a neutral, relaxed state of mind, then you can begin the assessment process.
